Bacon & Sausage Breakfast Burritos

Four hearty Ninja flat-top burritos with crisp bacon, plenty of browned sausage, tender eggs, grated sharp cheddar and a golden tortilla. We learned from our first batch: skip the soft hash browns, increase the sausage, and keep sauce subtle. Our next sauce experiment is a bright avocado-jalapeño-lime blend.

Mise en place

  • Preheat Ninja flat-top to 350°F. Set aside a cooler zone for the eggs if possible.
  • Shred sharp cheddar from the block; measure sausage into four equal portions for the original recipe.
  • Whisk eggs with a little salt and black pepper; remember that bacon and sausage already bring salt.
  • Set out plates, spatulas, a paper-towel-lined bacon plate, and tortillas.
  • Blend the avocado-jalapeño-lime sauce just before serving, but serve it separately so the burritos don't get soggy.
The flat-top does not need olive-oil cooking spray for bacon or sausage. Save a thin film of rendered bacon fat for the eggs and tortilla toast.

Sauce laboratory

Finding from batch 1: salsa verde plus straight sour cream overpowered the eggs and meat. Cholula alone was fine. Test very small amounts of sauce on the side, not inside the burrito.

Featured next test

Fresh avocado-jalapeño-lime sauce

Bright lime, fresh cilantro, a small jalapeño kick and smooth avocado. No mayo, sour cream, yogurt or extra oil; avocado provides the creaminess. This is a new sauce idea, not yet taste-tested with the burritos.

Blend the avocado, jalapeño, cilantro, lime, garlic, salt and the first 2 tbsp cold water until silky. Add water little by little until it drizzles. Taste; adjust lime for brightness, jalapeño for heat, and salt for balance. Optional zest adds freshness without additional sourness. Start with 1–2 tsp per burrito on the side. For mild kids' portions, omit jalapeño or serve the sauce separately. Best made fresh; if prepping ahead, press wrap directly against the surface, refrigerate and use promptly.

Alternative: light Gordita-style spicy ranch

The adapted version of our previous Cheesy Gordita Crunch ranch. It uses much less sour cream than the original and no jalapeño juice.

Whisk until smooth. Add a few drops of water or milk to make it drizzle. Rest in the refrigerator 15–30 minutes when possible. Start with 2 teaspoons per burrito for dipping, not flooding. We now favor testing the avocado-lime sauce first, since the previous salsa verde and sour cream were overpowering.

Cholula garlic mayo

Whisk smooth. Start with 1 teaspoon on the cut side of each burrito. No sour cream, and the hot sauce gives it just enough lift.

Simple Cholula

Literally a few dashes, the one you already thought was fine. No prep, almost no extra calories, and the filling stays the star.

Original Gordita Crunch spicy ranch (for reference)

120 g mayonnaise, 60 g sour cream, 20–30 g milk/buttermilk, 8–10 g ranch seasoning, 2 g paprika, 1 g chili/chipotle powder, 0.5–1 g cayenne and 1 g sugar. Optional hot sauce and a tiny squeeze of lime. Original correction: no jalapeño juice. This is the stronger historical sauce, not the adapted burrito version.

Kitchen journal · First batch → next batch

Actual test · Nov 1 First batch: 8 eggs, 8 bacon slices, 4 oz breakfast sausage, 2 oz cheddar, homemade shredded hash browns and four 10-inch tortillas. The potatoes were not dry enough and did not crisp especially well. Burritos were a little underfilled; salsa verde and sour cream obscured the filling. Cholula worked acceptably.

Proposed next test 10 eggs, 8 bacon slices, 8 oz breakfast sausage, 3 oz sharp cheddar, four 10-inch tortillas, no potatoes. Each burrito receives 2½ eggs, 2 bacon slices, 2 oz sausage and ¾ oz cheese. Optional full 1 oz cheese per burrito if the next batch still needs more melt.

Verdict to test next time: much more sausage, half an extra egg per burrito, ¾–1 oz sharp cheddar, no hash browns, and the new avocado-jalapeño-lime sauce as our next featured experiment (spicy ranch and Cholula remain alternatives). No Greek yogurt. Revision 2 is a planned adjustment, not yet taste-tested.

Troubleshooting & chef's scoring

  • Still underfilled: increase sausage or eggs, not potatoes.
  • Soggy tortillas: toast uncovered; keep sauce on the side.
  • Dry eggs: reduce griddle to 300–325°F; stop once fully set but still tender.
  • Greasy filling: drain bacon and sausage before wrapping; reserve only a little fat on the flat-top.
  • Sauce overwhelms: skip sour cream completely or use just a little Cholula.
  • Cheese not obvious: increase from ¾ oz to a full 1 oz per burrito.
